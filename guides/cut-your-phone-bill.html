Cut your phone bill in half: a practical playbook

Mobile.Direct editorial team. Updated 18 November 2026. 6-minute read.

Key takeaways

  • Switching to a budget brand on the same network is the biggest single saving.
  • Pay for the data you use, not the data you fear using.
  • Bring your own unlocked phone instead of financing a new one.
  • Re-check your plan every year.

Most households can cut their phone bill significantly without losing coverage. Work through these steps in order; the first three deliver most of the saving.

1. Find out what you use

Look at the last three months of data, minutes and roaming. Use our data calculator if you want a projection. Write down the number.

2. Move to a budget brand on the same network

Sub-brands and MVNOs run on the same towers as the big networks. For single lines and light to moderate users, the saving is often a third or more. See which brands run on which network in our carrier directory.

3. Right-size your data

If you use 8 GB, a 15 GB plan is enough. Unlimited plans make sense mainly above about 25–30 GB or for heavy hotspot use.

4. Keep your phone longer, or buy unlocked

Financed phones can hide in the monthly bill for years. Buying unlocked, or refurbished, separates the phone from the plan so you can switch freely.

5. Pay upfront for several months

Multi-month prepaid plans often cost noticeably less per month than paying monthly.

6. Stack discounts

Autopay, paperless billing, employer, student, senior and military discounts can each take a few dollars off per line.

7. Drop insurance you do not need

Many credit cards include phone protection when you pay your bill with them. Compare the deductible before keeping carrier insurance.

8. Use Wi-Fi calling and Wi-Fi data

Wi-Fi calling fixes weak indoor signal for free and saves data at home and work.

9. Combine lines fairly

A family plan lowers the per-line price. Use our bill splitter to divide the cost fairly by usage.

10. Ask your current carrier

Once you have a cheaper offer in hand, call your current carrier's retention team. They may match it, which saves you the switch.

Frequently asked questions

Will switching affect my credit?

Prepaid plans usually need no credit check. Postpaid applications may involve one.

Do I lose my number when switching?

No. You can port your number to the new provider in most countries.
