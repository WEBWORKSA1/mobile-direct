5G explained: bands, speeds and what you actually get

Mobile.Direct editorial team. Updated 18 November 2026. 5-minute read.

Key takeaways

  • Mid-band 5G gives the best mix of speed and coverage.
  • mmWave is extremely fast but only over very short distances.
  • Many networks still anchor 5G on 4G (non-standalone).
  • A 5G icon does not guarantee faster speeds than good 4G.

5G is the fifth generation of mobile networks. What you experience depends far more on which frequencies your carrier uses near you than on the 5G label itself.

Three kinds of 5G

TypeFrequenciesSpeedCoverage
Low-bandBelow 1 GHzSimilar to good 4GWide, good indoors
Mid-bandRoughly 1–6 GHz, including the popular 3.5 GHz range (band n78) and C-band (n77)Fast, often hundreds of MbpsCity and suburban
mmWave24 GHz and above (n257–n261)Very fastA block or less; blocked by walls

Standalone vs non-standalone

Non-standalone (NSA) 5G uses a 4G connection to manage the link while 5G carries data. Standalone (SA) 5G runs end to end on a 5G core, which enables lower latency and features like network slicing. Many networks run both.

Is 5G worth it?

  • If your carrier has mid-band 5G where you live, you will likely see a real speed gain.
  • If you mostly use Wi-Fi, 5G matters little.
  • 5G can use more battery in weak signal areas; most phones switch intelligently to save power.
  • Almost every phone sold today supports 5G, so you rarely pay extra for the phone.

Read your real signal

Bars on the status bar are a rough guide. Your phone's diagnostic screens show RSRP (signal power), RSRQ (quality) and SINR (signal to noise). Enter them in our signal interpreter to see whether slow speed is a signal problem or congestion.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my phone show 5G but feel slow?

You may be on low-band 5G, on a congested cell, or on a plan with lower data priority.

Do I need a new SIM for 5G?

Usually not. Some carriers require a newer SIM or eSIM profile for standalone 5G.
