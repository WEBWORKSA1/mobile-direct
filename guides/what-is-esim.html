eSIM explained: setup, transfer and travel

Mobile.Direct editorial team. Updated 18 November 2026. 6-minute read.

Key takeaways

  • An eSIM is a SIM built into the phone that you download instead of insert.
  • Dial *#06#: if an EID appears, your phone supports eSIM.
  • Most phones store several eSIMs and use one or two at a time.
  • Move your eSIM before you wipe or sell a phone.

An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a chip soldered inside your phone that can hold mobile network profiles. Instead of inserting a plastic card, you download your plan by scanning a QR code, opening the carrier's app or tapping a link. The phone then connects exactly as it would with a physical SIM.

Does my phone support eSIM?

The quickest test works on almost every phone: open the dialler and type *#06#. If the screen shows an EID (a long 32-digit number) alongside your IMEI, the phone has an eSIM chip. Most iPhones from the XR and XS onward, Google Pixel phones from the Pixel 3 onward, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later, and recent foldables support eSIM, but regional versions differ. Our compatibility checker covers the common models.

The phone also needs to be unlocked if you want to add an eSIM from a different carrier than the one that sold it.

Installing an eSIM

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi. The profile downloads over the internet.
  2. On iPhone, go to Settings, then Mobile or Cellular, then Add eSIM. On Android, go to Settings, Network and internet, then SIMs, and add an eSIM.
  3. Scan the QR code, use the carrier's app, or accept the eSIM your carrier pushes to your phone.
  4. Label the line (for example "Home" and "Travel") and choose which line handles data.

Keep the QR code private. Some can only be used once; a deleted profile may need a new code from the carrier.

Moving an eSIM to a new phone

Many carriers support transferring an eSIM directly between phones of the same platform during setup. If that is not available, ask the carrier for a new eSIM QR code for the new device. Do this before you erase the old phone, and never delete an eSIM profile you still need until the new one works.

Why eSIM is great for travel

You can keep your home number active for calls and verification texts while a travel eSIM handles data. You buy the plan before you leave, it activates when you land, and there is no airport SIM counter. Our eSIM planner estimates how much data a trip needs and lists providers to compare.

When a physical SIM still makes sense

  • Your phone or tablet has no eSIM chip.
  • You swap SIMs between devices often.
  • Your local carrier still charges for eSIM or handles it only in store.

Security

eSIM removes one theft risk: a thief cannot pop the SIM out to stop the phone being tracked. Protect your carrier account with a PIN, because a criminal who controls your account could request a new eSIM for your number.

Frequently asked questions

How many eSIMs can a phone hold?

Most phones can store several profiles, typically eight or more, and use one or two at the same time depending on the model.

Can I use an eSIM and a physical SIM together?

Yes, on dual-SIM phones that still have a SIM tray. Phones sold without a tray in some markets use two eSIMs instead.
