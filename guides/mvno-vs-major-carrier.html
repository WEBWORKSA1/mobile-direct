MVNO vs major carrier: what you really give up

Mobile.Direct editorial team. Updated 18 November 2026. 6-minute read.

Key takeaways

  • An MVNO rents capacity on a big network, so coverage maps are the same.
  • The main trade-off is data priority during congestion.
  • Check hotspot rules, roaming and phone compatibility before switching.
  • For single lines and light users, the saving is often 30–50%.

A mobile virtual network operator, or MVNO, is a phone company that does not own radio towers. It buys capacity wholesale from a network owner and sells plans under its own brand. Some MVNOs are independent; others are budget brands owned by the network itself, often called sub-brands or flanker brands.

Why MVNOs are cheaper

MVNOs skip the costs that make big carriers expensive: national store networks, heavy advertising, device subsidies and premium perks. Many run online only, with app-based support. The network owner sells them spare capacity because it earns money from towers that would otherwise sit idle at quiet times.

What stays the same

  • Coverage. If the MVNO runs on a network, you get that network's towers. Rural coverage is identical in normal conditions.
  • Your number. You can port your existing number in.
  • Your phone. Most unlocked phones work, as long as they support the host network's bands.

What can change

Data priority

When a tower is congested, for example at a stadium or a busy station at rush hour, networks decide whose data goes first. MVNO and prepaid customers are often placed behind the network's own premium customers. In daily use most people never notice; in crowded places, speeds can drop.

Hotspot limits

Some MVNOs cap tethering separately or slow hotspot speeds. If you work from a laptop on mobile data, read the hotspot terms first.

Roaming

Big carriers more often include roaming in neighbouring countries. Many MVNOs offer limited or paid roaming. If you travel, pair a cheap MVNO with a travel eSIM.

Support and extras

Expect app or chat support rather than a store. Perks like streaming bundles are less common, and phone financing may be limited.

How to switch safely

  1. Confirm your phone is unlocked and supports the host network's bands.
  2. Note your account number and request a transfer or port-out PIN from your current provider.
  3. Sign up with the new provider, choose to keep your number, and enter the details.
  4. Keep the old SIM until the port completes, usually within hours.

Frequently asked questions

Is an MVNO the same as prepaid?

Not exactly. Most MVNOs sell prepaid plans, but some offer postpaid billing. Network-owned brands also sell prepaid plans.

Will my 5G work on an MVNO?

Usually yes if your phone and plan both support 5G on the host network. Some plans limit the fastest 5G to premium tiers.
