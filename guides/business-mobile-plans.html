Business mobile plans: fleets, BYOD and device management

Mobile.Direct editorial team. Updated 18 November 2026. 6-minute read.

Key takeaways

  • Decide between company-owned phones and bring-your-own-device first.
  • Pooled data plans avoid paying for unused allowances.
  • Device management keeps company data safe on every phone.
  • Get at least two competing quotes; business pricing is negotiable.

Business mobile service is less about the cheapest plan and more about control: who owns the number, who owns the device, how data is protected and how costs are tracked.

Company-owned or bring your own device?

Company-ownedBYOD
ControlFull: the company owns the phone and numberPartial: work profile only
CostDevice and planMonthly stipend or partial reimbursement
SecurityEasier to enforceRelies on work profiles and app policies
Best forField teams, regulated industriesOffice staff, small teams

Plan structures

  • Per-line unlimited: simple, predictable, good for heavy users.
  • Pooled or shared data: one data bucket across all lines; efficient when usage varies.
  • Data-only lines: for tablets, hotspots, vehicles and sensors.

Device management

Mobile device management (MDM) lets you enrol phones automatically, push apps and Wi-Fi settings, enforce passcodes and wipe lost devices. Apple and Google both offer zero-touch enrolment programmes that work with MDM software, so new phones configure themselves on first boot.

eSIM for fleets

eSIM lets you provision or switch lines remotely, without shipping SIM cards. It is especially useful for remote staff and for moving devices between users.

Questions to ask every provider

  1. What is the total monthly cost per line after discounts, and how long is the term?
  2. Is data pooled, and what happens when the pool runs out?
  3. What roaming is included for staff who travel?
  4. Is there a dedicated account manager and business support line?
  5. What are the early termination terms?

Frequently asked questions

How many lines count as a business account?

Many carriers open business accounts from a single line with a registered business, though the best pricing usually starts at five or more lines.

Can employees keep their number when they leave?

Company-liable numbers belong to the company. Agree a porting policy in advance.
