How to choose a phone plan without overpaying

Mobile.Direct editorial team. Updated 18 November 2026. 7-minute read.

Key takeaways

  • Start with coverage where you actually spend time, then shop on price.
  • Measure your real data use before paying for unlimited.
  • Compare total cost over the contract, including price rises and fees.
  • Budget brands often use the same towers as the big networks.

Most people pick a phone plan once, then forget about it for years. That is exactly why so many overpay: plans change every few months, while the habits that decide which plan fits you change slowly. A good choice comes down to five questions asked in the right order.

1. Which network actually works where you are?

Coverage comes first because a cheap plan with no signal at home is not cheap. Every country has a small number of companies that own radio networks, and many more brands that resell access to them. Before comparing prices, find out which network is strongest at your home, your workplace and your commute.

  • Ask neighbours and co-workers which network they use and whether calls drop indoors.
  • Check a crowdsourced map built from real speed tests, not only the carrier's own marketing map.
  • If you can, borrow a SIM or try an eSIM trial on a second line for a week.

Once you know the best network, every brand that runs on it becomes a candidate. That usually multiplies your options.

2. How much data do you really use?

Open your phone's settings and look at mobile data used over the last few billing cycles. Most people land between 5 and 15 GB a month. Video is the big driver: standard-definition streaming uses around 1 GB an hour and HD can use about 3 GB an hour, while music streaming at high quality uses roughly 0.15 GB an hour.

If your usage is under 20 GB, a fixed-data plan is usually cheaper than unlimited. If you regularly use a hotspot for a laptop, or stream on the commute every day, unlimited starts to pay off. Our data usage calculator turns your habits into a monthly figure.

3. What is the total cost, not the headline price?

The advertised monthly price is rarely what you pay. Add these before comparing:

  • Annual price rises written into some contracts.
  • Taxes and regulatory fees, which some carriers include and others add on top.
  • Activation or connection fees.
  • Device payments bundled into the bill, and any promotional credits that disappear if you leave early.

Multiply the real monthly cost by the number of months you plan to stay, then subtract any trade-in value. The total cost calculator compares two options side by side, including yearly increases.

4. Which inclusions matter to you?

Inclusions are where plans differentiate once price and coverage are similar. Decide which of these you would actually use:

  • Hotspot data and whether it is capped separately.
  • Roaming in other countries, and whether it is included or sold by the day.
  • Priority data, which keeps your speed up when towers are congested.
  • Streaming subscriptions, cloud storage or device insurance bundled in.
  • Calling and texting to other countries.

Perks only count if they replace something you would otherwise pay for.

5. Which discounts can you stack?

Common discounts include paying by automatic payment, adding lines for family members, paying several months in advance, and employer, student, senior or military programmes. Multi-month prepaid plans often deliver the lowest effective monthly price if you can pay upfront.

Prepaid or postpaid?

Prepaid plans are paid in advance, usually need no credit check and are easy to leave. Postpaid plans are billed after use, often bundle phone financing and can include more perks. If you bring your own unlocked phone, prepaid is usually the better deal.

A simple decision rule

Your situationUsually the best fit
Under 5 GB, mostly on Wi-FiLow-cost prepaid plan on the strongest local network
5–20 GB, one or two linesBudget brand or MVNO with a mid-size data bucket
Heavy streaming or hotspot useUnlimited plan with a generous hotspot allowance
Frequent international travelPlan with included roaming, or a cheap home plan plus travel eSIMs
Family of four or moreMulti-line plan with per-line discounts

Frequently asked questions

Is unlimited data worth it?

Only if you regularly use more than roughly 25–30 GB a month or rely on a hotspot. Below that, a fixed-data plan is almost always cheaper.

Do budget brands have worse coverage?

They use the same towers as their host network, so coverage is the same. During congestion, their data may be slowed before the host network's own premium customers.

How often should I review my plan?

Once a year, or whenever your usage changes. Plan prices and promotions change several times a year.
